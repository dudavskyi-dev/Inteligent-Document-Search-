No manufacturer noise this time. Every party is a Veterans Affairs entity. On the seven-page fixture the same contract produced "York International" and "Toshiba" as parties; given the full document, the genuine contracting entities dominate and the chiller brands are correctly ignored. The party-definition weakness in my prompt is real, but it only bites when the document gives the model nothing better to latch onto.

The date is right; its timezone is not

The source reads OFFER DUE DATE/LOCAL TIME 07 - 21 - 2020. Parsing 07 - 21 - 2020 — with spaces around the separators — into 2020-07-21 is a genuinely good normalization, and the model correctly set timezone_source: "document_locale" rather than claiming an explicit zone.

But the emitted value ends in Z, which asserts UTC. Claiming local-time provenance while emitting a UTC instant is internally inconsistent, and a bid deadline that silently shifts by several hours is not a harmless error. My validator accepted it because datetime.fromisoformat parses it fine — it has no rule tying timezone_source to the offset in value. It should.

The document also gives no time of day, so midnight was invented rather than abstained on.

Two defects, one of them new

The three summary rows are the same over-extraction as the fixture run: 0001, 0002 and 0003 are group headers over their lettered sub-items. Consistent and predictable, and fixable in the prompt.

New this run: those three carry unit: "Do Not Quote". That is a price instruction that has been read as a unit of measure. JB, MO and EA are real units; "Do Not Quote" is not, and an ERP with a controlled unit list would reject it. My validator has no unit check — the blueprint calls for an allowed-unit list, and this is the case that needs it.

Page 25 was not parsed as a table at all — Docling emitted it as 52 loose text blocks. The evidence IDs prove it: page 24's items cite table rows (ev_p24_t12_r2) while page 25's cite plain blocks (ev_p25_b398). The model reassembled five CLINs with correct quantities and units out of unstructured text the parser had failed to lay out. That is a real robustness result, and it is the kind of recovery a rules-based extractor could not manage.

What the equipment inventory tells us

Pages 13 and 14 carry the chiller inventory — six York units with quantities, buildings and installation years. Both pages were sent to the model. Neither produced a line item.

On the seven-page fixture, the same inventory did become line items 0–5. Given the whole document, the model instead treated the contract line items on pages 24–25 as the line items and read the inventory as context.

That is arguably the better reading — in a solicitation, "line items" means CLINs. But it exposes a real ambiguity: line_items is doing double duty for "things being ordered" and "parts and models referenced", and Task 9 asks for parts and models as an extraction target in their own right. Under the current contract, the six chiller models are simply lost. The same structural gap as the NASA acceptance criteria, in a different place.

Retrieval selected page 14 but not page 13. The stitcher had already joined them into one logical table, so expansion pulled page 13 in. Without that step half the equipment inventory would never have reached the model. This is the first run where the stage changed the outcome.

Two pages the retriever should have found

Page 4 contains "The period of performance for this request is one year." It did not appear anywhere in the deadlines top-10. Your frozen qrels mark page 4 as relevant for the query "What is the period of performance for the chiller maintenance request?" — narrow and specific. My field-family query bundles three concepts:

"What are the submission deadlines, delivery dates, and periods of performance?"

Bundling dilutes the signal. Splitting deadlines into separate submission and performance-period families would likely recover it. This is a retrieval defect, independent of which model answers.

Page 15 holds the service-frequency table and never entered the parts_quantities pool either. Both misses are consistent with query wording rather than with the retrieval method, which found the price schedule and the solicitation header at rank 1.

A note on the page cap. The first attempt at this document capped at 12 pages and dropped 11, 13, 14 and 25 — the equipment inventory and half the price schedule. I had been truncating a page-sorted list, which discards high-numbered pages regardless of relevance. It now caps by retrieval priority and records what it dropped. This run used a cap of 18, so nothing was dropped at all.

Abstentions — finally declared

"abstained_field_paths": [
  "/line_items/unit_price",
  "/line_items/line_total",
  "/line_items/delivery_deadline"
]

Every price column in this solicitation is blank by design, and the model said so instead of silently returning nulls. Three of the four earlier runs left this array empty while omitting whole fields; this is the behaviour the contract was written to expect.

The paths are not valid JSON Pointers into the array — /line_items/unit_price should be /line_items/0/unit_price and so on for each index. The schema only requires non-empty strings, so validation passed. Worth tightening if these paths are ever consumed programmatically rather than read by a person.

Verdict and fixes

This is a usable extraction. A reviewer would accept nine line items and the bid deadline, delete three summary rows, fix one description and one unit, and correct a timezone. On a sixty-page solicitation that is a real saving over reading it manually.
