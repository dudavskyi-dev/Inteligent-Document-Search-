This document broke the contract, not the pipeline. NASA-STD-8739.14 is a procurement standard — it has no order lines, no quantities, no prices. Its substance is acceptance criteria: a fifteen-row lot-size sampling plan and a quality-control matrix on page 16, plus tensile and dimensional inspection rules on pages 14–15.

In extraction_candidate_v1.schema.json, tolerances exists only inside candidateLineItem. With zero line items there is nowhere to put any of it. The retriever found those pages, the parser captured both tables cleanly, the model was handed all of it — and the schema had no field to receive it.

The empty line_items is a success, not a miss. This is the failure mode I was watching for: page 7 carries a table of applicable documents — NPR 7120.5, NASA-STD-5019, NPR 7900.3 — which look superficially like part numbers in a two-column table. The model did not turn them into order lines. That is correct restraint.

But abstained_field_paths is empty again. Both /line_items and /deadlines were deliberate absences and should have been declared. That makes three runs out of four where the model returned nothing without saying so — the one exception being stealth/ox-alpha, which declared /deadlines on the GSA document.

DLA is the same class of error as York International on the chiller run: an organization named in the text as a reference — here, the authority maintaining the Qualified Suppliers List — rather than a party to the document. Milder, since DLA is at least a procurement body, but still not a party.

Verdict

The pipeline behaved correctly throughout. It routed, parsed, indexed, narrowed, and extracted without a single blocking issue, and it resisted the trap of turning a reference-document table into order lines. What it produced is accurate.

What it produced is also nearly useless for this document, and that is a contract problem with a clear fix.
